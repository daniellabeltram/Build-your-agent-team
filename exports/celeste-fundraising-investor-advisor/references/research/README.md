# Research Index: Independent Trade Publisher in West Palm Beach, FL

*Prepared 2026-11-20. Context for the expert agent team that comes next.*

## What we are researching

The goal is to open a book publishing house in West Palm Beach, Florida, modeled on how
**Penguin Random House (PRH)** operates: a traditional publisher that acquires manuscripts,
pays authors advances and royalties, edits and produces books, and sells them through
bookstores, online retailers and libraries.

> **Important framing:** PRH does not franchise or license its name, and "Penguin Random House"
> along with its imprint names are registered trademarks. You can't "open a Penguin Random House."
> These docs assume an **independent publisher run the PRH way**: curated imprints, editorial
> quality, trade distribution, and author advances. The company would have its own name. A later
> option is to sell to, co-publish with, or get distributed by a Big Five house (see
> [competitors](03-competitors.md)).

## Key takeaways

1. **The market is big, mature and slow-growing.** US publishing made **$33.4B in 2025** (+2.5%),
   and Trade made **$21.7B** (+2.7%). Print is still about 73–76% of trade revenue. Religious
   publishing is the fastest-growing trade segment (+9.3%). *(AAP StatShot Annual 2025)*
2. **Palm Beach County is a strong local base.** It has about **1.58M residents**, **26.1% are 65 or older**,
   and it is wealthy and highly educated. That means many book buyers, many would-be authors
   (memoir, business, lifestyle), and active philanthropy and event circuits.
3. **The model works on a list, not on a single book.** Per-title economics are thin: a typical
   title costs **$25k–$60k** and most titles lose money or break even. A list of **8–12 titles a year**
   lets a few hits pay for the rest. Expect to be cash-negative for **3–5 years**.
4. **Distribution is the gatekeeper.** Full-service distributors (IPS, PGW, IPG and others) usually
   want a track record or about **10+ titles**. Start on IngramSpark or a smaller distributor, then
   graduate.
5. **Regulatory burden is light.** You need an LLC or corporation on Sunbiz, an EIN, a Palm Beach
   County *and* a City of West Palm Beach Business Tax Receipt, Florida sales tax registration
   for direct sales, ISBNs, and copyright and contract hygiene.
6. **Top risks:** undercapitalization, distribution and returns, reliance on a few titles, Amazon
   concentration, AI-driven content glut, and Florida's book-challenge climate (relevant for
   children's and YA lists; see *PRH v. Gibson*).

## Recommended starting position (for the agent team to pressure-test)

- **Entity:** Florida LLC, which can later convert to a C-corp if outside investors come in.
- **List strategy:** 2–3 focused imprints. Candidates that fit the local market:
  - *Florida and coastal regional nonfiction:* history, nature, food, travel
  - *Business, leadership and finance:* draws on the Palm Beach executive and wealth-management community
  - *Upmarket fiction:* thrillers and coastal/beach reads with Florida settings
- **Volume:** 4–6 titles in Year 1, 8–10 in Year 2, and 12+ by Year 3 to qualify for top distributors.
- **Capital:** plan for **$500k–$1.2M** over the first 3 years (see cost doc).
