# 02 — Target Customers

[← Index](README.md)

A publisher has **three customer groups**. You must win all three.

## 1. Authors (supply side: who you acquire from)

| Segment | Why they'd pick us | How to reach them |
|---|---|---|
| **Mid-list authors** dropped or under-supported by the Big Five | Personal attention, faster schedules, higher royalty share | Literary agents (agents reach about 80% of quality manuscripts) |
| **Debut literary and commercial fiction writers** in Florida and the Southeast | Local house, editorial access | MFA programs (FAU, FIU, UCF, UM), writing conferences, agents |
| **Executives, founders and financiers** in Palm Beach | Credible traditional imprint (not vanity), business-book expertise | Networking, wealth managers, chambers, ghostwriters |
| **Subject experts** (Florida history, nature, food, real estate) | Regional distribution know-how | Museums, universities, historical societies |
| **Retiree memoirists** | Prestige and quality | Note: most memoirs are *not* commercially viable. Be selective, or route them to a separate services arm (see risks) |

**Agents are the real gatekeepers.** To be credible, the house must offer:
- Competitive (even if modest) advances: **$2k–$25k** for an indie (est.)
- Standard royalties: hardcover 10% to 5,000 copies, 12.5% to 10,000, then 15% of list. Trade paperback 7.5% of list.
  Ebook 25% of net (est. industry norms)
- Real distribution into bookstores, a marketing plan, and timely royalty statements

## 2. Trade buyers (who pays the invoices)

| Channel | Share of an indie's sales (est.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon** | 40–55% | Largest single account. Pushes for deep discounts |
| **Barnes & Noble** | 10–20% | Store-level curation has made B&N more open to indies since 2019 |
| **Independent bookstores** (via Ingram and the distributor) | 10–20% | Key for regional and literary titles. Local: Palm Beach Book Store, Books & Books (Coral Gables / Miami Beach), Barnes & Noble locations in WPB, Boca and Wellington |
| **Libraries** (via OverDrive/Libby, Baker & Taylor successor channels, Ingram) | 5–15% | Palm Beach County Library System is large and well funded. Library Journal and Booklist reviews matter |
| **Special sales / non-trade** | 5–20% | Museums, gift shops, corporate bulk buys (business books), associations, airports |
| **Direct to consumer** (own website, events) | 2–10% | Highest margin. Shopify plus a newsletter |

## 3. End readers (who actually reads the book)

Primary local and national personas to build imprints around:

1. **"The Palm Beach Book Club Member"**: female, 55–80, affluent, buys hardcovers, reads upmarket fiction,
   memoir and history. Influenced by luncheons, book clubs and local press (*Palm Beach Daily News* ("the Shiny Sheet"),
   *Palm Beach Post*, *Palm Beach Illustrated*).
2. **"The Snowbird / Florida Visitor"**: buys Florida-interest books as gifts and souvenirs. High seasonality.
3. **"The Ambitious Professional"**: 30–60, finance and tech workers who relocated to South Florida. Reads business,
   self-improvement and leadership titles. Consumes audio. Discovers books through podcasts and LinkedIn.
4. **"The Genre Binge Reader"** (national): romance, thriller and mystery readers who discover books through BookTok and newsletters.
   They prefer paperback and ebook, read in series, and are very loyal to authors.
5. **"The Bilingual Family"**: Hispanic families in South Florida who want bilingual and Spanish children's books.

## Implications
- Pick imprints so that **author supply (local) meets reader demand (national)**.
- Lead with business, regional and upmarket fiction. They fit the local author supply and the affluent readership.
- Genre fiction (romance and thriller) scales nationally but needs a series strategy and a social-first marketing engine.
