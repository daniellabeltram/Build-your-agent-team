# 03 — Competitors

[← Index](README.md)

Publishers compete in two markets at the same time: **for authors and agents** (acquisitions) and
**for shelf space and attention** (sales). Below are the competitors at each level.

## Tier 1: The Big Five (the model we're emulating)

| House | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Penguin Random House** (Bertelsmann) | Largest trade publisher in the world. 300+ imprints (Knopf, Viking, Crown, Riverhead, Ballantine, Berkley, etc.). Its scale advantages are distribution (it also distributes other publishers), backlist, and marketing data. Its proposed merger with Simon & Schuster was blocked in 2022 |
| **HarperCollins** (News Corp) | Owns Harlequin (romance) and Thomas Nelson / Zondervan (Christian, Nashville). Very strong in religious publishing |
| **Simon & Schuster** (KKR, since 2023) | Private-equity owned, so expect a cost focus |
| **Hachette Book Group** (Lagardère/Vivendi) | Includes Little, Brown, Grand Central and Orbit |
| **Macmillan** (Holtzbrinck) | Includes FSG, St. Martin's and Tor |

**How we compete:** We don't compete on scale. We compete on **attention per author, speed (9–12 months
from contract to publication versus 18–24), regional focus, and niche expertise.** The Big Five are also potential
**partners**: PRH Publisher Services and Simon & Schuster Distribution distribute independents.

## Tier 2: Successful independents (the realistic benchmarks)

| Publisher | Why it's a useful model |
|---|---|
| **Sourcebooks** (Naperville, IL) | Largest woman-owned indie. Built on data-driven marketing and romance. Proof that an indie can scale outside NYC |
| **Chronicle Books** (San Francisco) | Gift, design and regional strength |
| **Workman** (now Hachette) | Built by sustaining backlist evergreen titles |
| **Graywolf / Milkweed / Coffee House** (MN) | Nonprofit literary model, funded by grants and donors |
| **Sourcebooks Casablanca, Entangled, Bloom** | Romance-focused indies with series strategies |
| **Pineapple Press** (Florida; now part of Globe Pequot / Rowman & Littlefield) | **Closest direct analog.** Founded in Florida in 1982, focused on Florida-interest books. Its acquisition shows there is an exit path for a regional list |
| **University Press of Florida** (Gainesville) | Scholarly and regional Florida titles. Competes for Florida-history authors |

## Tier 3: Local and Florida competitors

| Competitor | Location | Model | Threat level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ballast Books** | **West Palm Beach** | Hybrid (author-funded) publisher, founded 2017 | **High** for executive and memoir authors in our backyard |
| **CRC Press** (Taylor & Francis) | Boca Raton | Scientific and technical | Low (different market) |
| **Indigo River Publishing** | Pensacola | Independent, its own imprint | Medium |
| Palm Beach Publications & Media, *Palm Beach Illustrated* / Hour Media, VIVE | Palm Beach County | Magazines and coffee-table books | Low–medium (coffee-table and lifestyle books) |
| Various "publishing services" firms | South Florida | Pay-to-publish / vanity | Low on quality, but they confuse the market and compete for the same affluent authors |

## Tier 4: Self-publishing and platforms

- **Amazon KDP** and **IngramSpark**: authors can publish for close to $0. Top self-published genre authors earn more
  than most traditionally published mid-listers. Our value proposition has to be clear: **editorial quality,
  bookstore and library placement, reviews, rights sales and prestige.**
- **Amazon Publishing imprints** (Thomas & Mercer, Montlake, Lake Union): dominant in genre fiction ebooks.

## Competitive positioning (draft)

> *"A Palm Beach–based traditional publisher with Big Five standards and boutique attention.
> Our books reach every bookstore, library and screen, and we never charge authors to publish."*

**Differentiators to build:**
1. Traditional model (no author fees), in clear contrast to local hybrids
2. Editors with Big Five pedigree, recruited from NYC (remote or relocated to Florida)
3. Florida and Sun Belt regional sales expertise, plus a national genre or business imprint
4. Speed to market and transparent royalty reporting (an author portal)
5. Strong local events platform (Palm Beach luncheon and charity circuit, Miami Book Fair presence)

## Open questions
- Is it better to **acquire an existing small Florida list** (for an instant backlist) than to start from zero?
- Should we offer a separate, clearly branded **author-services arm** for cash flow, with no conflict with the trade imprint?
