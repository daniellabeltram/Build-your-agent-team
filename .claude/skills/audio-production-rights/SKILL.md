# Audio production & rights

*Roster skill S19. Preloaded into: `audio-publishing-producer`, `production-manufacturing-manager`, `subsidiary-rights-specialist`.*

## Purpose
Digital audio is the growth format (docs/01). Decide case by case whether to **produce** or **license**.

## Decision frame
| Option | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| **License** to an audio publisher (e.g. a major audio imprint or specialist) | Advance paid up front, no production cost, their marketing | Smaller share of revenue (publisher usually splits license income with the author) |
| **Produce in-house** and distribute (aggregators/retailers, library platforms) | Highest margin, own the asset | ~$3k–$10k per title upfront (est.), QA burden, marketing on us |
| **Royalty-share production** | Low upfront | Lower quality control, constraints |

## Cost drivers (est.)
Finished hours ≈ words ÷ 9,300. Narrator rates are often quoted **per finished hour (PFH)**: about $250–$500 for an experienced narrator, more for top or union talent. Add studio, editing and mastering, proofing, and cover adaptation.

## Method
1. Estimate the audio demand (genre: thrillers, romance, business, and memoir over-index on audio).
2. Get a license offer if possible and compare it with an in-house P&L over 3 years.
3. For production: cast (auditions), contract (work-for-hire, credit), record, proof (listen-through against the text), master to spec, and deliver.
4. Plan for AI narration questions. Disclose it, check author consent, and know retailer policies.

## Pitfalls
- Selling World audio rights cheaply on a potential lead title.
- Forgetting to secure audio rights in the author contract.
