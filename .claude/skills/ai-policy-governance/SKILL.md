# AI policy & governance

*Roster skill S21. Preloaded into: `ai-emerging-tech-advisor`, `editorial-acquisitions-director`, `media-law-counsel`, `publishing-contracts-attorney`.*

## Purpose
AI is both a threat (content glut, copyright uncertainty) and an opportunity (efficiency, licensing revenue) (docs/08 risk #8).

## Policy components
1. **Author side:** a contract warranty that the work is human-authored, with disclosure of any AI assistance. Prohibit undisclosed AI-generated text and images. Publisher rights to request process evidence.
2. **Internal use:** an approved-tools list. Allowed uses (research, metadata drafts, marketing copy drafts, accessibility alt-text drafts, with human review). Prohibited uses (uploading unpublished manuscripts to tools that train on inputs; AI covers without disclosure). Data-handling rules.
3. **Narration and translation:** require author consent, disclose to consumers, and follow retailer policies.
4. **AI training licensing:** opt-in only with author consent and revenue share. Evaluate price, scope, attribution, and reputational impact. Track litigation and Copyright Office guidance.
5. **Brand:** consider a "human-made" commitment as a differentiator.

## Method
1. Identify the decision (clause, tool, or licensing offer).
2. Assess legal status (sources current within 6 months), contractual rights, author sentiment, and revenue.
3. Recommend with conditions, and route contract language to publishing-contracts and media counsel.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming copyright protects AI-generated material. The US Copyright Office requires human authorship.
