# Manuscript evaluation & list curation

*Roster skill S08. Preloaded into: `brand-marketing-strategist`, `editorial-acquisitions-director`, `subsidiary-rights-specialist`.*

## Purpose
Taste plus market discipline. Decide what to publish and how it fits the list.

## Evaluation rubric (score 1–5 each)
1. **Writing quality / voice**: is it publishable at Big Five standard?
2. **Hook / concept**: can it be pitched in one sentence?
3. **Market**: are there 3–5 recent comparable titles (≤3 years old) with real sales?
4. **Author platform**: audience, media, network, and for nonfiction, credentials.
5. **Imprint fit**: does it match an imprint's promise?
6. **Edit lift**: how much work is required (developmental vs. polish)?
7. **Series / backlist potential**: will it keep selling or lead to more books?
8. **Rights potential**: foreign, audio, film/TV.
9. **Risk**: legal (libel or permissions), reputational, or content-challenge exposure (Florida HB 1069 context for children's and YA).

## Method
1. Read the query, synopsis, and sample (or the proposal for nonfiction). Note your first impression honestly.
2. Score the rubric and write a 1-paragraph reader's report.
3. Pull comps and expected sales range, then request a title P&L (title-pl-unit-economics skill).
4. Recommend: **Pass / Revise & Resubmit / Acquire**, with a suggested advance range and publication season.

## List balancing
Each season, aim for 1–2 lead titles, a mix of formats, at least one evergreen/backlist builder, and no more than ~40% of spend on one category. Check the list against capital limits.

## Pitfalls
- Falling for local prestige over national market.
- Vanity pressure from wealthy local authors. Taking an author's money is a different business (see docs/08 risk 11).
