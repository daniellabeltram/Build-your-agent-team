# Publishing industry data synthesis

*Roster skill S01. Preloaded into: `brand-marketing-strategist`, `engagement-coordinator`, `market-competitive-analyst`, `publishing-strategy-advisor`, `risk-insurance-advisor`.*

## Purpose
Turn raw industry statistics into insights that support a decision. Always tie a number back to what it means for a small, PRH-style independent house in West Palm Beach.

## Baseline facts (from docs/01-market.md)
- US publishing revenue was $33.4B in CY2025 (+2.5%). Trade was $21.7B (+2.7%). *(AAP StatShot Annual 2025)*
- Adult fiction $7.1B (+3.9%), adult nonfiction $6.0B (+1.3%), children's/YA fiction $5.4B (+0.9%), religious $2.2B (+9.3%).
- Trade print is about 73% of revenue (HC ~$8.0–8.2B, PB ~$7.8–8.0B). Digital is ~21.5%. Ebooks are flat at ~$1B, and digital audio is the growth format.
- The AAP monthly panel showed trade -0.5% YTD 2025. Treat the market as **mature and share-shifting**.
- Palm Beach County: 1,575,726 people (2025), 26.1% aged 65+, 26.1% Hispanic. *(Census QuickFacts)*

## Method
1. State the question the data must answer (e.g. "Is the religious segment big enough for an imprint?").
2. Pull the most recent authoritative figure. Note its source, year, and whether it comes from the **annual estimate** or the **monthly reporting panel**. These differ, so never mix them.
3. Normalize: same year, same scope (total vs. trade vs. segment), and nominal vs. real.
4. Translate the figure into venture terms: what share would a 10-title list need, and what does growth or decline mean for advances or print runs.
5. Flag uncertainty and how it changes the recommendation.

## Source hierarchy
1. AAP StatShot (publisher net revenue) and Circana BookScan (print unit sell-through, ~85% of retail)
2. Publishers Weekly, Publishers Lunch, Publishing Perspectives, The Bookseller
3. US Census, BLS, BEA for local demographics and wages
4. Vendor and blog estimates. Use only as labeled estimates.

## Output
A short insight block: **Finding → Source (year) → So what for us → Confidence (High/Med/Low)**.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing publisher revenue (net of discounts) with consumer spending (list price).
- Treating BookScan units as complete. It misses many library and special sales.
- Quoting a growth rate without its base year.
