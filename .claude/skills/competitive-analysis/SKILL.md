# Competitive analysis & positioning

*Roster skill S03. Preloaded into: `brand-marketing-strategist`, `editorial-acquisitions-director`, `market-competitive-analyst`, `publishing-strategy-advisor`.*

## Purpose
Understand who we compete with **for authors** (acquisitions) and **for readers and shelf space** (sales), and find positions we can win.

## Competitive set (from docs/03-competitors.md)
- **Big Five:** PRH, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, Hachette, Macmillan. They are the model and potential distribution partners.
- **Indie benchmarks:** Sourcebooks, Chronicle, Graywolf/Milkweed (nonprofit model), **Pineapple Press** (Florida regional, acquired), University Press of Florida.
- **Local:** **Ballast Books** (WPB hybrid, author-funded) is the main local threat for executive and memoir authors. CRC Press (Boca, STM) is low threat. Vanity and services firms muddy the market.
- **Platforms:** Amazon KDP, IngramSpark, and Amazon Publishing imprints.

## Method
1. Define the arena: an acquisition category or a sales category.
2. For each competitor, profile the model (traditional, hybrid, or self), categories, list size, distribution, advance level, marketing strength, and weakness.
3. Map competitors on two axes relevant to the arena (e.g. author cost vs. distribution reach; national vs. regional focus).
4. Identify white space: under-served authors, categories, or regions.
5. Write a positioning statement: *For [target], [house/imprint] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative], because [proof].*
6. List the proof points we must build to make the position credible.

## Output
A competitor table, a positioning map (described in text), white-space bullets, the positioning statement, and the proof points.

## Pitfalls
- Don't try to compete with the Big Five on scale or advances. Compete on attention, speed, niche, and region.
- Don't copy PRH trademarks or imprint names.
