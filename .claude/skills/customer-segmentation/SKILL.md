# Customer segmentation & personas

*Roster skill S04. Preloaded into: `brand-marketing-strategist`, `digital-social-marketer`, `editorial-acquisitions-director`, `market-competitive-analyst`, `special-sales-d2c-manager`, `trade-library-marketing-specialist`.*

## Purpose
A publisher has three customer groups: **authors** (supply), **trade buyers** (who pay invoices), and **readers** (who read). Segment each one so the team can make decisions about them.

## Existing segments (docs/02-target-customers.md)
- **Authors:** mid-list authors dropped by the Big Five; debut Florida writers; Palm Beach executives and financiers; subject experts; retiree memoirists (be selective).
- **Trade channels (est. share):** Amazon 40–55%, B&N 10–20%, indies 10–20%, libraries 5–15%, special sales 5–20%, direct-to-consumer 2–10%.
- **Reader personas:** Palm Beach Book Club Member; Snowbird/Florida Visitor; Ambitious Professional; Genre Binge Reader; Bilingual Family.

## Method
1. Choose the segmentation basis: needs, behavior, channel, or demographics. Prefer needs and behavior.
2. Size each segment with a cited proxy (Census, AAP category size, channel share).
3. For each persona, write: who they are, what job the book does for them, where they discover books, preferred formats and price, objections, and the triggers that make them buy.
4. Rate attractiveness (size × fit × reachability) and pick primary and secondary targets.
5. Translate the targets into decisions: imprint, format, price, channel, and message.

## Output
A segment table (size, attractiveness, priority) plus 1-page personas with "implications for us."

## Pitfalls
- Personas must lead to different decisions, or they're decoration.
- Don't confuse author supply (local) with reader demand (national).
